# 2026-04-11 Farcaster Mini App Poster Redesign

## What Changed

- Reworked the Farcaster Mini App poster surfaces to follow the `frontend-skill`
  poster-first rules instead of screenshot-replica or empty-gradient drafts.
- Reframed the preview asset around a single calm stage with short step rows,
  rather than a hollow centered card, then replaced that stage with a report-card
  layout that mirrors the project's real task-and-conclusion UI.
- Reworked the loading asset into a small avatar-style badge so it reads as a
  brand mark, not a blown-up screenshot or decorative image.
- Removed the promo surface's top-left metrics strip and KIKO pill so the hero
  title owns the left side cleanly.
- Kept the promo and social surfaces in the same brand family with sparse copy,
  strong contrast, and one primary visual anchor each.
- Regenerated the final local renders at:
  - `/Users/almurat/KiKo/output/farcaster-brand-v4/preview-shot-5.png`
  - `/Users/almurat/KiKo/output/farcaster-brand-v4/promo-shot-4.png`
  - `/Users/almurat/KiKo/output/farcaster-brand-v4/loading-shot-4.png`
  - `/Users/almurat/KiKo/output/farcaster-brand-v4/social-shot-3.png`

## Why

Earlier drafts were too close to generic screenshots or too empty to feel like
finished product posters. The new direction treats each surface as a composed
brand artifact:

1. first viewport = poster, not app shell,
2. one dominant anchor per image,
3. sparse copy with readable hierarchy,
4. loading = avatar-first brand mark,
5. preview = report-card or chat-first poster when the content is conversational.

## Guardrail

Do not reintroduce screenshot replicas, collage-style mockups, or dense UI
microcopy into the Farcaster promo assets. Any further iteration should preserve
the poster-first composition and the brand mark as the strongest object.
